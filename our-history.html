Our History & Values

PAGL is a Protestant and unionist-oriented forum examining language policy, cultural identity and political life in Northern Ireland. We welcome respectful contributions from people of every background, including those who disagree with us.

Protestant history in Northern Ireland

Protestant communities have shaped the region's religious, industrial, social and political history. Unionism developed in part around maintaining Northern Ireland's place within the United Kingdom. Experiences differ widely across Protestant communities, and neither unionism nor Protestant identity is a single viewpoint.

Stormont, 1921–1972

The Northern Ireland Parliament operated from 1921 until its suspension in 1972. Its unionist-led governments faced sustained criticism for discrimination in housing, voting arrangements and public employment, alongside conflict over constitutional identity. Some unionists refer to this period as the “Orange State”; that phrase is politically contested. Direct rule from Westminster followed in 1972 and the old Parliament was abolished in 1973.

Concerns since the end of the old Stormont system

Some Protestant and unionist voices argue that their traditions and concerns have received insufficient recognition in debates about parades, flags, public culture and constitutional change. Those concerns deserve careful examination through specific policies, decisions and evidence. Others emphasise that reforms were necessary to address historical discrimination and protect equal rights. PAGL invites documented accounts and fair debate rather than assuming every disputed decision amounts to discrimination.

Drumcree and parading

The annual Drumcree dispute in Portadown centres on an Orange Order parade route and objections from Garvaghy Road residents. Orangemen describe the importance of longstanding parading traditions and freedom of assembly. Residents and their supporters raise concerns about consent, safety and equality. Decisions by the Parades Commission and the courts form part of the history. We aim to record developments with dates and links to primary sources.

For current determinations and official information, visit the Parades Commission and the Northern Ireland government information service.

Political outlook

PAGL describes its outlook as right-of-centre and broadly unionist. The group's stated position is supportive of Nigel Farage. It previously supported Donald Trump but now expresses disappointment with him. These are PAGL's political opinions, not endorsements by every member or contributor.

Everyone is welcome to debate

We welcome people of all religions and political viewpoints. No foul language, personal insults, sectarian abuse or threats. Challenge arguments with evidence and courtesy; disagree firmly, but fairly.

Historical discussion is presented for context. Commentary reflects attributed perspectives and should not be mistaken for findings of fact without evidence.
