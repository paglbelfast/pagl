Who we are

The PAGL campaign

PAGL stands for Protestants Against Gaelic Language. This website brings together our language-policy campaign, political commentary, Drumcree coverage, the history of opposition to compulsory Irish and Protestant remembrance.

We oppose policies that give Irish-language qualifications unnecessary weight in education or employment, and spending on language promotion that we believe should go towards essential services. We welcome supporters of every faith and none. Our campaign uses peaceful argument and democratic participation.

Our position

Choice. Equal access. Spending priorities.

End compulsion

We want Irish to be a matter of individual choice. Students and families should be able to prioritise subjects that suit their needs and ambitions.

Remove unnecessary barriers

We oppose Irish-language requirements where they are unrelated to the actual work. Ability to do the job should come first.

Challenge promotion costs

We oppose expanding public spending on Irish-language promotion. Housing, healthcare, education and essential services should take priority.

PAGL position · Education

A language should win support through choice

Making people study Irish is not the same as persuading them of its value. PAGL argues for a voluntary approach that respects students who want to learn other subjects. We want schools to recognise different abilities and ambitions, rather than treating Irish-language study as a test of belonging.

PAGL position · Employment

Judge applicants on the work they will do

Language requirements should face a clear question: does the job genuinely require this skill? PAGL opposes blanket preferences and unnecessary qualifications that narrow opportunities for people who do not speak Irish. We want recruitment to be based on relevant skills, experience and the needs of the service.

PAGL position · Public money and signs

Put practical services before language promotion

We challenge spending on Irish-language promotion, translation and additional signage where we believe practical benefits do not justify the cost. Our position is that public information should be clear and useful, and that essential services deserve priority. Any spending figures we publish will identify the organisation, year and activity being counted.

Choosing your own language

I started thinking about something that seems to drive people mad — the Irish language.

Now, before anyone reaches for the pitchforks, hear me out.

Imagine a good old Irish family sitting around the kitchen table.

Mam speaks English.
Dad speaks English.
The kids speak English.
Grandad speaks English.

Then someone walks in and says:

“Right lads, you’re Irish — so here’s the language you’re supposed to speak.” 😂

Mam looks at Dad.

Dad looks at Mam.

Grandad probably asks if there’s tea.

And the whole family carries on speaking the language they’ve always spoken.

That’s the funny thing about language.

You can put it on signs.
You can teach it in schools.
You can spend millions promoting it.

But you can’t manufacture something that people don’t naturally choose to use.

And that’s really my point.

I’m not telling anyone they can’t love Irish. If you love it, learn it, speak it and teach it to your children. That’s your choice.

But surely the same freedom should apply to the person who doesn’t want to?

Good old Irish people should be allowed to choose their own language without being told that their choice makes them less Irish.

Anyway… I think there’s a song in this somewhere. 😂

What do you think?

And if you disagree, tell me why — I’m genuinely interested in hearing the argument.

Our background and principles

Protestant History & PAGL Values

As Irish Protestants, we have a long history on this island, and we are proud of it. Since the old Stormont Parliament came to an end, many unionists have felt that our traditions and concerns are too easily dismissed. We want to talk openly about that, especially when it comes to parades, flags, identity and equal treatment. We know others see that history differently, and they are entitled to say so.

Our outlook

We are unionist and centre-right. We support Nigel Farage. We once supported Donald Trump too, but we have been disappointed by him. We will say what we think, even when that means disagreeing with politicians we previously backed.

Open to everyone

PAGL is strictly non-sectarian. We welcome and support Roman Catholics as well as Protestants, and we already have a Roman Catholic member. We welcome people of every faith and none. We will argue our case strongly, but there is no place for sectarianism, racism, threats, foul language or personal abuse.

History and Drumcree

Drumcree matters deeply to many unionists. We believe our traditions and the right to parade deserve a fair hearing. We also recognise that residents have rights and concerns. Our aim is to argue our position without attacking anyone for who they are.

From our X posts · September–October 2026

PAGL on politics, language and the Union

These pieces put our recent social media arguments into fuller words. They express PAGL's political opinions, not established findings about other people's motives. We oppose bigotry and judge people by their actions, not their nationality or religion.

9 October 2026 · Westminster and immigration

Who represents Britain in Parliament?

We want every MP to uphold the interests of their constituents, respect the United Kingdom and take their parliamentary duties seriously. We also want an honest debate about immigration, border policy and democratic accountability. We oppose judging an MP's loyalty by their ancestry or nationality: representatives should be assessed on what they say and do. Claims about how any community will vote should be supported by evidence, not assumptions.

7 October 2026 · Irish politics

We can disagree with a government and still recognise its achievements

We strongly disagree with what we see as the Irish government's approach to Northern Ireland and Protestant concerns. But we can still give credit where we believe it is due. The Republic's economic success and the role of corporation tax deserve serious discussion. We can praise economic policy while challenging constitutional or cultural policy. That is not a contradiction; it is independent thinking.

26 September 2026 · Parades and signage

Respect for Protestant parades and fairness over public signs

We believe Protestant traditions, including lawful parades, deserve respect. We also question decisions to introduce Gaelic-language signs in shared public spaces such as Belfast transport hubs. For us, both matters raise questions of identity, consent and fair treatment. We recognise that others value Gaelic signage and that residents have rights too. The answer is open debate, clear rules and equal treatment under the law, not insults or intimidation.

21 September 2026 · Everyday language

English belongs to all of us

English is a shared everyday language for Protestants and Roman Catholics, across Northern Ireland and the Republic, and for people of many backgrounds around the world. We believe public services should be easy for everyone to understand and that no one should face unnecessary language barriers. That does not mean people should be prevented from speaking or learning Gaelic. Our argument is against compulsion and what we see as unfair policy, not against speakers.

18 September 2026 · The constitutional question

A border poll must be fair to both sides

We believe any future referendum on Northern Ireland's constitutional status must follow the law and its result must be respected by unionists and nationalists alike. We question political positions that appear to treat the obligation to accept a result differently depending on who wins. That is our criticism, not a claim that any party is legally exempt from the outcome. The same democratic rules must apply to everyone.

4 September 2026 · The 1973 border poll

Remembering the 1973 referendum

The 1973 Northern Ireland border poll produced a large vote in favour of remaining in the United Kingdom among those who voted. Nationalist parties encouraged a boycott, so the result must be understood alongside the turnout and political circumstances. We believe the referendum belongs in any serious account of Northern Ireland's constitutional history, while recognising that the Good Friday Agreement now provides the framework for future constitutional change.

17 September 2026 · Rule of law and whistleblowers

Fair treatment for whistleblowers and defendants

We are concerned by allegations that people who speak out about wrongdoing can face unfair treatment. Where Garda whistleblowers or anyone else alleges retaliation or wrongful prosecution, the claims deserve proper independent scrutiny. The same principle applies regardless of a person's politics: accusations should be tested by evidence, courts must be independent, and nobody should be declared guilty or innocent simply because we like or dislike them.

Northern Ireland

Unionist perspectives and Drumcree

This is where we speak about Northern Ireland from a unionist point of view, especially Drumcree. We will be plain about our opinions and link to the facts behind them.

Drumcree · Background

Parading rights, residents' concerns and the Garvaghy Road

Drumcree remains a significant issue in discussions of Orange Order traditions, freedom of assembly, local residents' rights and policing. Unionist participants emphasise the cultural and religious importance of the parade; Garvaghy Road residents have objected to the route and raised concerns about community consent. PAGL supports lawful advocacy for parading traditions and fair treatment in decisions about routes.

Northern Ireland · Commentary

Stormont, identity and political representation

We will comment on decisions that affect the Union, our traditions and everyday life in Northern Ireland. We will not pretend every unionist agrees with us, and we will not shy away from disagreement. When we add news or commentary, we will date it and show our sources.
